For families

A first step.
A place to belong.

You can begin with curiosity. The skills come through trying things together.

Meet your team

Cubs belong to a Pack and work in small teams called Sixes. Scouts belong to a Troop and work in Patrols. Games and practical activities turn a group of newcomers into a team.

Learn by doing

An evening can include a game, a new skill, a shared challenge and a chance to reflect. You practise, ask questions and gradually take on more responsibility.

Arrange a visit

Cubs meet Mondays, 18:00–19:30; Scouts meet Wednesdays, 18:00–20:00. Contact Donovan before coming to Sunderland Street Scout Hall and check any changes or school breaks.

Ask about joining, costs, what to bring and any support that would help your child settle in. You can discuss uniform before buying it.

Cubs · Ages 7–10

Small discoveries.
Growing confidence.

The Cub Trail gives games, skills and helping others a direction.

  1. 01

    Membership

    Get to know the Pack, its traditions and the Cub Promise and Law before investiture.

  2. Silver Wolf

    Explore the Awareness, Community, Outdoor and Aptitude challenges, alongside Promise and Law.

    See earning guide →

  3. Gold Wolf

    Build on those same areas with the next set of challenges and new opportunities to try things for yourself.

    See earning guide →

  4. Leaping Wolf

    From age ten, Cubs can work towards this highest Cub award with their Pack Scouter.

    See earning guide →

  5. Link Badge

    As moving to Scouts approaches, meet the Troop and prepare for the next part of your journey.

    See earning guide →

Interest badges give you other subjects to explore along the way. Your Pack Scouter helps choose suitable goals and confirms the requirements and achievements.

Scouts · Ages 11–17

Try a skill.
Grow into a leader.

Advancement is a personal journey through practical experience, service and responsibility.

  1. 01

    Membership

    Discover Scouting’s background and the Scout Promise and Law as you join the Troop.

  2. Traveller

    Develop your first outdoor, safety and Scout skills while taking part in your Patrol.

    See earning guide →

  3. Discoverer

    Strengthen your skills and begin supporting others as you develop leadership.

    See earning guide →

  4. First Class

    Take more responsibility for planning and organising adventures and service with your team.

    See earning guide →

  5. Springbok

    Work towards South Africa’s highest Scout award through demanding leadership and personal challenges.

    See earning guide →

Six themes weave through the journey

  • Safety Awareness
  • Living Outdoors
  • Adventure
  • Scout Skills
  • Service
  • Personal Development

Choose a next step with your leader, practise it and arrange the right assessment. Being at an activity is different from demonstrating a requirement. This public guide does not record or award advancement.

The Promise & Law

Values you can
put to work.

Our everyday choices matter just as much as the skills we learn.

For Cubs

The Cub Promise centres on doing your best, duty to God and country, following the Pack’s Law and a daily good turn. In practice: listen to guidance, keep trying and look for a small way to help.

For Scouts

The Scout Promise brings together personal effort, duty to God and country, helping others and living by the Scout Law. Ask your leader to teach you the exact words for investiture.

Ten ways to put the Scout Law into action

Our plain-language reminders below explain the ideas; they are not the official wording of the Law.

Be dependable

Keep a promise and tell the truth.

Stand by others

Support your team when things get difficult.

Offer help

Notice a useful job and lend a hand.

Welcome people

Include someone who is new to the group.

Show respect

Listen carefully and speak considerately.

Care for animals

Treat living creatures gently.

Follow guidance

Carry out instructions responsibly.

Keep your spirit

Face a setback with patience and good humour.

Use things wisely

Care for equipment and avoid waste.

Choose well

Take responsibility for thoughts, words and actions.

Try together: choose one reminder and share an example at the end of your next evening.

For grown-ups too

Your experience.
Someone’s next adventure.

You do not need to know every knot before starting a conversation about volunteering.

Share an interest

Tell us what you enjoy, from practical skills to creative activities. Discuss how a suitable session could support the programme.

Find a realistic role

Talk about the help the group needs and the time you can offer. Helping behind the scenes and exploring leadership are different ways to get involved.

Learn and be supported

SSA offers adult training and mentoring. A leader can explain the application process, required checks and training for the role you are considering.
